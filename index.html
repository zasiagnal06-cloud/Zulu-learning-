"I see you" — how a conversation in Zulu begins

The everyday greetings and phrases you'll actually hear across KwaZulu-Natal, with pronunciation, so you can use them from the first day.

Before you start

Zulu (isiZulu) is tonal and every letter is pronounced — there are no silent letters to trip over. The trick is in the rhythm: stress usually falls on the second-to-last syllable of a word.

A note on "hello": isiZulu has separate words depending on how many people you're greeting. Get this right and you'll already sound like you know what you're doing.
Greetings
Sawubona sah-woo-BOH-nah
Hello — said to one person. Literally "I see you."
Sanibonani sah-nee-boh-NAH-nee
Hello — said to a group of people.
Unjani? oon-JAH-nee
How are you? — asked to one person.
Ninjani? nee-njah-nee
How are you? — asked to a group.
Ngiyaphila ngee-yah-PEE-lah
I'm fine / I'm well — the standard reply.

Everyday phrases
Ngiyabonga ngee-yah-BAW-ngah
Thank you.
Yebo YEH-boh
Yes.
Cha chah
No.
Ngicela... ngee-CHEH-lah
Please, may I have... — used to politely ask for something.
Igama lami ngu... ee-GAH-mah LAH-mee ngoo
My name is... — fill in your name at the end.
Uphuma kuphi? oo-POO-mah KOO-pee
Where are you from?
Saying goodbye

isiZulu splits farewells by who is leaving and who is staying — using the right one shows you've paid attention.

Hamba kahle HAHM-bah KAH-shleh
Go well — said to the person who is leaving.
Sala kahle SAH-lah KAH-shleh
Stay well — said to the person who is staying behind.
